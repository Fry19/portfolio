Bridging Health Equity: Accessible Healthcare for Women and Minority-Owned Businesses

Bridging Health Equity is a service to make healthcare more accessible and tailored for women and minority-owned businesses. By addressing critical barriers such as affordability, complexity, and inclusivity, the platform empowers underrepresented communities with personalized healthcare solutions.

Problem Statement

Women and minority business owners often face unique challenges in accessing affordable and customized healthcare. Existing platforms are not designed with these groups in mind, often featuring unrealistic price plans, jargon-heavy content, and limited options. Bridging Health Equity was created to bridge these gaps by offering a streamlined, user-friendly solution tailored to their needs.

Target Audience

The primary users of BHE are women and minority business owners who seek affordable and accessible healthcare plans for themselves, their families, and their employees. These users value clarity, simplicity, and inclusivity in their healthcare experiences.

My Role

As the UX Researcher and UX Designer, I was responsible for conducting in-depth user research, competitive analysis, developing personas and journey maps, creating wireframes and prototypes, and leading usability testing. Collaborating closely with developers and healthcare experts, I ensured that user needs remained central throughout the design process.

02

Process

Understanding the User

The project began with extensive user research. I conducted 10 interviews and surveys with women and minority business owners to uncover their pain points and needs. Stakeholder interviews with healthcare experts further informed our understanding of the challenges users face. Key insights from this phase revealed that users struggled with complex healthcare terminology and plan comparison processes. Many expressed a need for a platform that was simple, transparent, and visually inclusive.

I next created a competitive analysis to get a better understanding of information, layout, and other design elements that our competition was using so we could improve upon their shortcomings and produce an all-around more accessible and intuitive product.

Personas

Using the insights gathered, I developed user personas like Maria, a Latina entrepreneur seeking affordable family healthcare, and James, an African American startup owner focused on employee wellness. Journey maps were created to visualize their experiences, highlighting critical frustrations such as confusion around plan eligibility and difficulty navigating existing platforms.

01 / 02

Generating Solutions

During the ideation phase, the team brainstormed features to address user pain points. The focus was on creating a seamless plan comparison tool and simplifying complex healthcare jargon through tooltips, These ideas were refined into actionable design solutions.

Bringing Ideas to Life

The design process began with low-fidelity wireframes to establish the basic layout and navigation structure. High-fidelity wireframes followed, incorporating a modern aesthetic with accessible typography, clear call-to-action buttons, and visuals representing the diverse user base.

01 / 03

Results

Refining the Experience

Usability testing was conducted with 5 target users. Feedback revealed opportunities to improve navigation flow, simplify explanations for healthcare terms, and enhance visual clarity. Based on this input, the team iterated on the design, adding tooltips for complex terms, refining the visual hierarchy, and optimizing the navigation experience.

The usability testing aimed to evaluate the user experience of the BHE platform and identify areas for improvement. The target users participated in the test. These users were tasked with comparing healthcare plans, navigating to bilingual support options, and signing up for an account. The sessions were conducted remotely, and observations focused on understanding their pain points, ease of navigation, and overall satisfaction.

The testing revealed that the navigation flow was unclear, particularly when users switched between comparing healthcare plans. Many participants struggled with understanding complex healthcare terminology, which slowed their progress and led to frustration. The visual hierarchy also needed improvement, as important information was not prioritized, making it difficult for users to quickly scan and digest content.

To address these issues, the team restructured the navigation for a more intuitive flow and added breadcrumb trails to enhance orientation. Tooltips and a glossary feature were introduced to clarify healthcare terminology, ensuring users could easily understand the information presented. The visual design was refined by updating color contrast, font sizes, and spacing to improve readability and accessibility.

The usability test underscored the importance of user feedback in creating a platform that meets the specific needs of women and minority business owners.

Outcome

The final design for BHE successfully addressed user needs. Testing showed a 40% increase in sign-ups. Additionally, the platform received an 85% satisfaction score in usability tests, with users highlighting its simplicity and inclusivity.

Challenges

One of the biggest challenges was simplifying the complex information surrounding healthcare plans while ensuring the platform remained informative. By breaking down information into digestible pieces and using interactive features like tooltips, we struck the right balance. This project emphasized the importance of empathy and iteration in the design process. It taught me that designing for underserved groups requires ongoing collaboration, listening, and adaptability.

Conclusion

BHE exemplifies the transformative power of user-centered design in addressing social inequities. The project was a rewarding experience that allowed me to apply my skills in research, design, and testing to create a meaningful solution for a critical issue.
