At a glance

The problem

People with celiac disease need safe food away from home, but finding it takes time and effort, and cross-contamination remains a real risk.

The approach

I ran user interviews and a competitive analysis, designed and usability-tested a mobile app, then extended the design across screen sizes, from smartwatch to desktop.

The outcome

High-fidelity mobile and desktop prototypes; testing led to clearer gluten-free labels and a Favorites tab. One participant said the app brings “the confidence to travel more and try new restaurants!”

01

Overview

This one is personal: I set out to make finding a safe meal quick, low-risk, and low-stress for the celiac community.

Background

OhYum! is a tribute to my mother, to myself, and to everyone living with a restrictive diet because of an autoimmune disease like celiac disease.

Challenge

Cross-contamination is a real risk in restaurants and other eateries, so eating out with celiac disease takes constant vigilance and extra care with every food choice.

Solution

Uber Eats, reimagined for the celiac community. A curated database surfaces low-risk meals nearby, taking the stress out of dining out or ordering in. It helps users find safe places to eat quickly, cutting wait times and the risk of cross-contamination.

02

Research

Two things kept people on edge: the time it takes to find safe food, which every interviewee raised, and the fear of cross-contamination at new restaurants.

Interviews

Takeaway: users needed a dedicated celiac app with precise language and reliable search, built to ease the challenges below and boost their confidence.

Preliminary research and pre-testing conversations shaped my interview questions.

User Pain Points

Availability

Food that’s safe, fast, and convenient is scarce, which makes it hard to balance access with dietary safety.

Risk

Even restaurants that advertise gluten-free options made many users anxious. Trying a new one meant worrying about cross-contamination and getting sick.

Time

Every interviewee raised it: finding and choosing safe food away from home takes time and effort, often delaying meals and adding stress.

Persona & Problem Statement

Juanita needs a quick, reliable way to find safe places to eat without risking illness.

She has celiac disease and loves traveling and spending time with friends and family, so trustworthy dining options are essential.

User Journey Map

The map traces Juanita’s ideal flow step by step, so I could make sure each stage of her experience felt easy.

03

Competitive Analysis

Existing apps left gaps, and those gaps became the starting point for my first sketches.

I studied Find Me Gluten Free, Uber Eats, and OpenTable as direct and indirect competitors, documenting their key screens with detailed notes and screenshots.

04

Ideate & Design

Juanita needed speed and reliability, so search results and map screens got most of my sketching time.

Paper Wireframes

Working from the problem statement, I sketched a range of solutions to the gaps I’d found in the competitive analysis, focusing on search results and map screens.

Digital Wireframes

Building on the paper sketches, I focused the first digital designs on personalized guidance that helps users meet their specific dietary needs.

05

Usability Testing

Testing surfaced what was missing: users wanted labels that clearly separate gluten-free from gluten-sensitive options, and they struggled to save favorite restaurants.

Low-Fidelity Prototype

I connected the full user flow, from browsing nearby restaurants to adding a food item to the cart, so testers could navigate the app as real users would.

Findings

Clear Labeling

Users asked for accurate restaurant labels that tell gluten-free apart from gluten-sensitive, so they can make informed choices and dine more safely.

Saving Favorites

Adding a new favorite restaurant to an account was a struggle. Users asked for favorites to live directly in the navigation bar.

06

Iterate & Refine

Both findings went straight into the mockups: one change for safer choices, one for faster access to favorites.

Mockups

  • Labeling: a distinct home-screen section clearly labels gluten-free vs. gluten-sensitive options.
  • Favorites: a new Favorites tab in the navigation bar gives quicker access.

09

Reflection

My biggest lesson was restraint: a big problem doesn’t need a big app. The next step is research into whether OhYum! actually helps people find safe food faster.

Even when a challenge feels vast, I don’t need to solve everything at once. Tackling one problem at a time gives users a more enjoyable experience, without unnecessary features to overwhelm them.

I loved building something that can make a meaningful difference for a community.

Next Steps

  • Research how well the app helps people with celiac disease find food faster and more safely.
  • Explore partnerships with restaurants on their gluten-free menus.
  • Add a reviews tab and section to the mobile app and web platform.
