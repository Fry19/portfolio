At a glance

The problem

People with celiac disease spend time and effort finding safe food away from home, and many feel anxious trying new restaurants that advertise gluten-free options for fear of cross-contamination.

The approach

I ran user interviews and a competitive analysis, designed and usability-tested a mobile app, then extended the design across screen sizes, from smartwatch to desktop.

The outcome

High-fidelity mobile and desktop prototypes; testing led to clearer gluten-free labels and a Favorites tab. One participant said the app brings “the confidence to travel more and try new restaurants!”

01

Overview

This one is personal. Eating out with celiac disease means weighing cross-contamination risk with every food choice, so I set out to build an Uber Eats for the celiac community: quick, safe, and low-stress.

Background

This project is a tribute to my mother, to myself, and to others living with restrictive diets because of autoimmune diseases like celiac disease. OhYum! helps people with celiac disease find safe, reliable food options tailored to their needs. Its intuitive interface and curated database make it easy to find low-risk meals nearby, taking the stress out of dining out or ordering in. Think of it as Uber Eats reimagined for the celiac community: convenience, safety, and peace of mind for anyone managing the condition.

Challenge

Eating out with celiac disease takes constant vigilance. Cross-contamination is a real risk in restaurants and other eateries, so every food choice has to be made with extra care.

Solution

An app that helps users quickly find safe places to eat, cutting wait times and minimizing the risk of cross-contamination and illness.

02

Research

Every interviewee raised the same pain point: finding safe food away from home takes too much time and effort. That, plus fear of cross-contamination, told me the app needed precise language and strong search.

Summary

Preliminary research and pre-testing conversations shaped the targeted questions behind my user interviews. One pain point came up with every interviewee: the time and effort it takes to find and choose safe food away from home. Many participants also described anxiety about trying new restaurants that advertise gluten-free options, for fear of cross-contamination. The takeaway was clear: users needed a dedicated celiac app with precise language and robust search, built to ease these challenges and boost their confidence.

User Pain Points

Availability

Safe, fast, and convenient food options are scarce for people with celiac disease, making it hard to balance accessibility with dietary safety.

Risk

Many users described feeling anxious about visiting new restaurants that advertise gluten-free options, worried about cross-contamination and getting sick.

Time

Users were frustrated by how long it takes to find safe food, which often delays meals and adds to their overall stress.

Persona & Problem Statement

Juanita has celiac disease and needs a quick, reliable way to discover safe places to eat without risking illness. She loves traveling and spending time with friends and family, which makes trustworthy dining options essential.

User Journey Map

I created a user journey map to outline the ideal flow for a user like Juanita and make sure each step of her experience felt intuitive.

03

Competitive Analysis

Existing apps left gaps, and those gaps shaped my first sketches. I went through the key screens of Find Me Gluten Free, Uber Eats, and OpenTable to find them.

I analyzed Find Me Gluten Free, Uber Eats, and OpenTable as key direct and indirect competitors. Detailed notes and screenshots of their key screens gave me insights that shaped my design decisions.

04

Ideate & Design

Juanita needed a quick, reliable way to find safe places to eat, so my sketches focused on search results and map screens. I built the first digital wireframes from there.

Paper Wireframes

Working from the problem statement, I sketched a range of solutions to the gaps I’d identified in the competitive analysis. I focused on improving search results and map screens to make the experience more intuitive.

Digital Wireframes

Building on my ideation and paper sketches, I created the initial digital designs for OhYum!, focused on delivering personalized guidance that helps users meet their specific dietary needs.

05

Usability Testing

Users wanted labels that clearly separate gluten-free from gluten-sensitive options, and they struggled to save favorite restaurants. I found both by testing the full flow, from browsing nearby restaurants to adding an item to the cart.

Low-Fidelity Prototype

To prepare for usability testing, I built a low-fidelity prototype that connects the full user flow, from browsing nearby restaurants to adding a food item to the cart, simulating how users would navigate the app.

Findings

Clear Labeling

Users wanted clear, accurate restaurant labels that distinguish gluten-free from gluten-sensitive options, so they can dine more safely and make informed choices.

Saving Favorites

Users struggled to add new favorite restaurants to their accounts and asked for favorites to live directly in the navigation bar.

06

Iterate & Refine

Both findings went straight into the mockups: gluten-free and gluten-sensitive options now get their own clearly labeled home-screen section, and Favorites sits in the navigation bar where users asked for it.

Mockups

Based on the usability testing findings, I added a distinct home-screen section that clearly labels gluten-free vs. gluten-sensitive options, and introduced a Favorites tab in the navigation bar for quicker access.

Accessibility Considerations

09

Reflection

My biggest lesson: a big problem doesn’t need a big app. Solving one problem at a time spares users unnecessary features, and the next step is measuring whether OhYum! actually helps people find safe food faster.

I loved working on this project. Creating a product that can make a meaningful difference for a community is rewarding in itself. My most valuable lesson: even when a challenge feels vast, I don’t need to solve everything at once. Tackling one problem at a time gives users a more enjoyable experience, without unnecessary features to overwhelm them.

Next Steps

  • Research how well the app meets its goal of helping people with celiac disease find food faster and more safely.
  • Explore partnerships with restaurants on their gluten-free menus.
  • Add a reviews tab and section to the mobile app and web platform.
